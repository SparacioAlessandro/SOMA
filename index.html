Bridging Equity and Rigor in Digital Mindfulness

Standardized Open-source Mindfulness Architecture (SOMA): a modular library of matched mindfulness–control script pairs for mechanistically precise RCT research.

🎯

Mechanistic Rigor

Every mindfulness asset is paired with a structurally equivalent Active Control, enabling isolation of bottom-up vs. top-down mechanisms of change.

🌍

Health Equity

Scripts validated at Grade 5–6 reading level with resource-neutral instructions — no quiet space, no special equipment, no prior experience required.

🧩

Radical Modularity

Themes (attitudes) are formally decoupled from practices via a Universal Bridge, enabling dismantling studies, longitudinal rotation, and flexible experimental design.

The SOMA Architecture

Three layers, 76 distinct assets

SOMA is not a single script library — it is a modular system. The building blocks combine to produce a far larger experimental toolkit than any fixed-session protocol.

🏗️

Thematic Intros (attitudes, ~2 min)

6 psychological attitudes — Being, Curiosity, Patience, Acceptance, Letting Go, Gentleness — each in two structurally equivalent A/B variations. These frame how a participant engages with the practice.

6 attitudes × 2 variations = 12 intros
🧘

Sensory Practices (10 min)

16 unique practice modules spanning formal seated meditation and informal everyday activities. Each is the primary "active ingredient" — the attentional task that the thematic attitude frames.

16 Mindfulness + 16 Control = 32 practices
🔗

Universal Bridge (10–20 sec)

A standardized, resource-neutral transition linking any thematic intro to any practice. Identical across both conditions — the "glue" that enables the library's radical modularity.

Enables: any Intro → any Practice pairing

The Conditionality

Mindfulness vs. Active Control

Every asset is doubled for scientific rigor. The control condition is not passive — it is an analytically structured counterpart matched on all non-specific factors.

🧘

Mindfulness Condition

Directs attention to bottom-up experiential sensing. Participants notice, observe, and experience without categorising or analysing.

"Notice the sound without labeling it."
🔬

Active Control Condition

Directs attention to top-down analytical categorisation. Same duration, same structure, same vocal prosody — differing only on the proposed mechanism.

"Identify the sound and classify its source."

Development Process

How SOMA Was Built

The SOMA library was developed through an iterative, multi-stage process combining expert committee review, AI-assisted auditing, and synthetic persona stress-testing.

👥 Expert Committee Design

An international committee of mindfulness researchers, clinicians, and psycholinguists collaboratively developed the script content, structural equivalence standards, and pedagogical ordering. Each script went through multiple rounds of review against the committee's operationalised criteria for mechanism specificity and accessibility.

🐍 AI-Assisted Script Auditing

Scripts were audited using a Python pipeline that called the GPT-4o API with a prompt designed to simulate a clinical psychologist and methodologist. This prompt contained a structured set of operationalised criteria — covering mechanism specificity, attitudinal language, blinding integrity, and banned phrasing — against which each script was evaluated. The AI audit is documented as a development aid, not a formal verification procedure, given the well-known limitations of large language models regarding reproducibility and hallucination. All findings were subsequently reviewed by the expert committee.

🤖 Synthetic Persona Simulation

As an exploratory developmental tool, a Python script called the GPT-4o API to simulate responses from synthetic participant personas varying across SES, personality, and prior attitude toward mindfulness. This exercise provided early qualitative signal about potential differential engagement across script types and helped flag areas for committee review. Given the substantial limitations of LLM-simulated human responses, all findings were treated as illustrative heuristics rather than empirical data, and fed back into the committee's revision process rather than informing any formal analytical decision.

📖 Cognitive Scaffolding & Tier Design

The 16 practices are organized into a three-tier cognitive scaffold based on working memory and interoceptive demands. Tier 1 establishes basic attention anchoring; Tier 2 integrates practice into daily routine; Tier 3 addresses advanced interoception and affective observation. Researchers may use the tiers sequentially as a clinical curriculum or decouple them entirely for experimental purposes.

Cognitive Scaffold

Three-Tier Practice Architecture

The 16 practices are organized into three tiers based on their inherent working memory and interoceptive demands. Tier 1 (Foundational) minimises cognitive load via immediate, concrete sensory anchors. Tier 2 (Core Extension) builds on these anchors within the context of daily routines. Tier 3 (Specialized) requires higher working memory capacity and finer-grained attentional control. Researchers may follow this sequence as a scaffolded curriculum or decouple tiers entirely for experimental designs.

Principle 1: Structural Equivalence

Unlike passive waitlist controls, SOMA Active Controls are matched to mindfulness scripts on five critical structural dimensions, eliminating common confounds including expectancy, instructor attention, and session burden.

DimensionStandard Applied
DurationIdentical ~12 min commitment across all 32 scripts
ProsodyRecorded by the same instructors with matched vocal pace (~100 wpm)
ArchitectureIntro–Bridge–Practice structure maintained across conditions
Practice BurdenMatched daily practice and homework requirements
Reading LevelBoth conditions validated at Grade 5–6 (Flesch–Kincaid)

Principle 2: Mechanistic Orthogonality

The Active Control is designed as Analytical Training. While mindfulness cultivates direct bottom-up sensing, the control directs top-down categorisation and analytical verification of the same sensory objects. This design is intended to produce a double dissociation: participants in the mindfulness condition should score higher on the State Mindfulness Scale (SMS — a measure of in-the-moment experiential awareness), while both conditions score equivalently on the Credibility/Expectancy Questionnaire (CEQ). The Active Engagement Scale (AES) captures the analytical engagement specific to the control condition, completing the predicted cross-over interaction.

Verb Substitution Protocol

Mindfulness: "Notice the sensation without labeling it."
Control: "Identify the sensation and name its location precisely."

Principle 3: Intervention Design Rationale

The SOMA library operationalises the consensus that mindfulness is not a monolithic construct, but a synergistic combination of attentional regulation and specific attitudinal qualities (Bishop et al., 2004; Shapiro et al., 2006). Each module pairs a top-down thematic framing (the Introduction) with a bottom-up attentional task (the Practice).

The thematic toolkit spans a pedagogical spectrum. Being and Curiosity establish basic sensory grounding and encourage non-judgmental exploration of routine stimuli, reducing automatic affective evaluation. Patience and Acceptance develop distress tolerance and the capacity to sustain attention without experiential avoidance. Letting Go and Gentleness train cognitive flexibility, decentering from mental fixations, and self-compassionate affect.

Formal vs. Informal Practices

Formal practices (e.g., body scans) systematically train sustained interoceptive awareness. Informal practices (e.g., handwashing, brushing teeth) embed mindfulness into daily routines — disrupting behavioral automaticity without increasing time-burden. Both are critical for ecological validity and accessibility in high-stress or low-resource populations.

Principle 4: Equity by Design

Accessibility is a foundational constraint in SOMA, not a post-hoc analysis.

Linguistic Accessibility

All scripts refined to Grade 5–6 reading level (Flesch–Kincaid) to ensure inclusion across diverse socioeconomic backgrounds without differential comprehension.

Resource Neutrality

Instructions explicitly welcome environmental noise and common interruptions. The "quiet space" prerequisite — which systematically excludes participants in dense urban or multi-occupancy settings — is eliminated by design.

Principle 5: Validation Study Design

The SOMA validation study is a pre-registered randomised controlled trial (N=360) designed to test three confirmatory hypotheses about the library's structural equivalence, mechanistic specificity, and universal accessibility. The study is being submitted as a Stage 1 Registered Report.

Full details of the study design, power analyses, pre-registration, analysis scripts, and stimuli are openly available on the project's OSF repository.
