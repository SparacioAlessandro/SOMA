The SOMA Approach

How the library is structured

Each session combines a 2–3 min Thematic Introduction with a 10 min Sensory Practice, matched to a structurally equivalent Active Control counterpart.

Principle 1: Structural Equivalence

Unlike passive waitlist controls, SOMA Active Controls are matched to mindfulness scripts on four critical structural dimensions. This eliminates common confounds including expectancy effects, instructor attention, and session burden.

DimensionStandard Applied
DurationIdentical 10–12 min commitment across all 32 scripts
ProsodyRecorded by the same instructors with matched vocal pace (~100 wpm)
ArchitectureIntro–Bridge–Practice structure maintained across conditions
Practice BurdenMatched daily practice and homework requirements
Reading LevelBoth conditions validated at Grade 5–6 (Flesch–Kincaid)

Principle 2: Mechanistic Orthogonality

The Active Control is designed as Analytical Training. While the mindfulness condition cultivates direct bottom-up sensing, the control condition directs top-down categorisation and analytical verification of the same sensory objects.

Critically, control scripts are engineered to differ on the proposed mechanism (Sensorimotor Subscale of the SMS) while being matched on non-specific factors (CEQ expectancy composite).

Verb Substitution Protocol

Mindfulness: "Notice the sensation without labeling it."
Control: "Identify the sensation and name its location precisely."

This produces the predicted double dissociation: the mindfulness condition should score higher on the State Mindfulness Scale (SMS) while both conditions score equivalently on the CEQ expectancy composite — validating blinding integrity and mechanistic specificity simultaneously.

Principle 3: Equity by Design

Accessibility is a foundational constraint in SOMA, not a post-hoc analysis. Two specific barriers are addressed by design:

Linguistic Accessibility

All scripts refined to Grade 5–6 reading level (Flesch–Kincaid) to ensure inclusion of participants from diverse socioeconomic backgrounds without differential comprehension.

Resource Neutrality

Instructions explicitly welcome environmental noise and common interruptions, removing the "quiet space" prerequisite that systematically excludes participants in dense urban or multi-occupancy settings.

Hypothesis H3 tests whether the SOMA intervention achieves its equity goal via a SES × Condition ANOVA and TOST equivalence test on the Semantic Differential composite.

AI-Assisted Development

SOMA scripts underwent a two-layer AI audit prior to finalisation. This is documented as a development aid, not a formal verification procedure, to avoid reproducibility issues arising from LLM non-determinism.

A synthetic persona stress-test (N=256, balanced 2×2×2 SES/Personality/Attitude grid, run with GPT-4o-mini) confirmed the AES vs. SMS double dissociation design and identified seven control scripts with critical attentional object overlap — triggering the Attentional Object Shift redesign protocol.

Validation Study Design

The SOMA validation study is a pre-registered RCT (N=360) targeting three confirmatory hypotheses, submitted as a Stage 1 Registered Report to Nature Human Behaviour.
